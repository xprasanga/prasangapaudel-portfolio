About the portfolio

A little about me

I am Prasanga Paudel, a PhD student in Agriculture and Applied Economics at Texas Tech University, with a growing interest in the intersection of economics, data, technology, and real-world decision making. My academic work has given me a strong foundation in economic theory, econometrics, statistics, and applied research, while my projects have pushed me to think more broadly about how data can be organized, analyzed, visualized, and communicated. This portfolio is a place where those interests come together. It is not intended to be only a collection of finished assignments or polished figures; it is also a record of how I am learning, experimenting, and developing as a quantitative researcher and programmer.

Much of my current work is rooted in applied economics. I am particularly interested in questions where economic behavior, public policy, environmental issues, energy, agriculture, and individual decision making overlap. My research experience has involved designing surveys, working with experimental information treatments, estimating willingness to pay, conducting econometric analysis, and interpreting results for policy-oriented audiences. I enjoy the process of taking a broad question, turning it into something that can be measured, building an empirical strategy around it, and then using data to understand what the results actually tell us. That process has also made me increasingly interested in the tools behind the analysis, rather than treating software as something that simply produces a final table or figure.

R and Quarto have been an important part of that development. I have used R for statistical and econometric analysis, data manipulation, visualization, and reproducible research, and I built this portfolio using Quarto. Working with Quarto has helped me see how analysis, writing, code, and presentation can be connected into one workflow. I have also started using Python, particularly for visualization and data-related projects, and I am now expanding my programming skills beyond the tools I have traditionally used for statistical analysis. My goal is to become comfortable moving between languages depending on the problem: using R when it is the right environment for statistical work, Python for data science and broader programming applications, and eventually C++ and Java for deeper programming and computational skills.

This website reflects that transition. Some sections represent work I have already completed, while others are deliberately placeholders for projects I will build in the future. I want the portfolio to show progression rather than pretending that every skill is already mastered. The programming section will eventually contain separate areas for Python, R, C++, and Java, with projects, exercises, visualizations, and applications organized within each language. The project section approaches the same body of work from another direction, allowing visitors to explore by subject area such as economics, finance, data science, and visualization. Over time, I would like each area to become its own small dashboard containing the projects, methods, code, results, and lessons associated with that topic.

I also care about presentation. Good quantitative work should be understandable to someone who did not write the code that produced it. For that reason, visualization and communication are important parts of the way I approach projects. A model, dataset, or statistical result becomes much more useful when its purpose and implications are clear. I am interested in building dashboards and visual explanations that make quantitative information easier to explore without removing the underlying complexity. This is one reason I am continuing to develop both my programming and web-design skills alongside my academic training.

Ultimately, this portfolio is a work in progress. My academic background is in agriculture and applied economics, but I see data and programming as increasingly important tools for working across disciplines. I want to keep building projects that combine economic reasoning with careful empirical analysis, useful visualizations, and reproducible code. As I learn new languages and techniques, this site will grow with me. The goal is not simply to demonstrate that I can use a particular software package; it is to show how I approach problems, how I learn new tools, and how I turn data into something that can support research, communication, and better decisions.
